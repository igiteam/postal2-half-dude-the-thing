[img[scalable mesh|process.png]]\n\n//"...playing co-op or deathmatch with a bunch of mates over Xbox Live, while seeing their actual faces on their actual characters is a huge technological leap forward for multiplayer gaming."// ([[Spong|http://news.spong.com/article/11039]])\n//"It was actually very easy to implement. ...all we're doing is running Digimask and generating a mesh that's unique ... So it's just a kind of plug and play thing for us."// (Ubisoft on Rainbow Six: Vegas)\n//"I can definitely see Digimask becoming a must-have feature for every create-a-character mode henceforth."// ([[Xbox 360 Fanboy|http://www.xbox360fanboy.com/2006/09/21/word-series-of-poker-first-impressions]])\n\n''User experience''\nThe Launch Pad program must first be loaded into the PC. The user takes 1 or 2 photos of their head (one face-on, one side-profile) and optionally marks several points on the photos as directed. Within a a few seconds their 3D Head is created, complete with 'virtual muscles' allowing full animation for speech, expression and deformation if required. The high-resolution head is then saved on the hard drive for use/importation into any supported game. The above process is a one-off as once the Head is saved, it can be used with any Digimask-enabled game.\n\n''Behind the scenes''\nBy incorporating the SDK library into a game, developers are able to import the DMK head into their game engine at runtime. The DMK data is a compressed, platform independent and proprietary format. Essentially all the SDK does is to decode this data for the developers game engine. The developer passes in the DMK data, the SDK reveals the 3D data. They can then request a DAG with polygon and texture data at whatever resolution suits them. For animation, the heads use influence skinning (bones). This information is then stored by the developer in their own scene-graph. Once all the data is extracted the SDK library can be shut-down (releasing any memory used) and the game can continue without any interference from the SDK.\n\nThere are two steps to the Digimask process:\n#Creation. The Digimask Head is created from a front and optionally a side photo of a person's head. This process happens in what we call the [[DCP|Digimask Creation Process]] or Digimask Creation Process. The [[DCP|Digimask Creation Process]] library is integrated into our Launch Pad application.\n#Integration. Once a Digimask Head has been created it is saved into a propriatary (DMK) format. This data can then be passed to the [[SDK]] for decoding and integration into your application.\n\nFor more information, see:\n[[SDK]]\n[[Digimask Creation Process]]\n[[Games Development]]\n[[Other Development]]
aka ''DCP''\nThe DCP is the process that turns the photographs into the full 3D model of the person's head. There are several versions of the DCP suitable for different situations.\n\nThe main distinction is between the ''manual'' and ''automatic'' processes. In manual mode, the DCP requires the user to mark some key points on the photos for certain feature points (for example the corner of the eye, mouth, etc). With the automatic version, no marked points are required, and apart from the person's gender and age range, all that is required are the photos. //The manual process is quicker once points have been marked (as little as 15 seconds), but more input (and hence user instruction) is required than in automatic mode.//\n\n||!Front photo|!Side photo|!Marking required|!Style of head|\n|!Manual front photo only|yes|no|yes|Monomask (front only)|\n|!Manual front and side photo|yes|yes|yes|Full head|\n|!Automatic front photo only|yes|no|no|Monomask (front only)|\n|!Automatic front and side photo|yes|yes|no|Full head|\n|Manual and Automatic DCP comparison|c\n\nA Monomask Head has all the same features as a full Digimask Head (animation and so on), except the back of the head is missing.\n\nThe DCP is integrated into all Digimask's products on several platforms including (but not limited to):\n*x86, ~PowerPC, MIPS CPU\n*Windows (x86)\n*Xbox 360\n*Mac OSX\n*Linux\n*~PS2

Handy Hints and Tips for making the Best Digimask Head for Xbox 360\n\n#You must have a neutral expression, eyes open, mouth closed. No smiling!\n#Ensure that the front photo is fully face-on. One way to check this is to see that both ears are equally visible. Too many shadows and bright highlights may create distortion. [img[scalable mesh|devel_01.gif]]\n\nDependent on the application for which you are developing, Digimask has a range of SDK materials which will assist with that designated platform.\n\nNote that the SDK is specific to the output file format of the [[DCP|Digimask Creation Process]]. Therefore, the SDK will allow you access to all of the various components of the Digimask proprietary 3D Head Model. For example:\nThe Mesh - The Texture - The 'Bones' and associated weights - All other data attributes\nThe mesh is also fully-scaleable thereby lending it suitable for any device employing a 3D graphics capability.\nNote that only the PC platform the DCP library itself does not feature in the SDK, although we can provide the facility for you to customise the Launch Pad application is you wish to.\n\nIf you are developing for a consumer-based application, then the publishing license will, of course, include free distribution of the ~DCP-based client.\n\n*''What is the API like?'' We use a easy to use C API. The DMSDK API is a very simple, efficient and stable API. Sample code and documentation is included.\n*''How many polys in the head?'' Polygon data can be extracted at any resolution (within reason). Around 100 polygons is a sensible minimum, 7000 or so will give you more than enough detail. A value in-between likely to be more practical\n*''What about the skin texture, how big is that?'' Skin texture can be requested at any resolution. Again, to suit the game engine and / or situation.\n*''How fast is your renderer?'' What renderer? That's not our job. We just give you the 3D data, it's your job to render it.\n*''How long does is take to retrieve the head?'' Data extraction is very fast. It only takes a couple of seconds to extract all the 3D data necessary for rendering. Requesting lower ~LODs doesn't take any longer.\n*''Can I animate the head?'' Digimask heads have a rich set of features for animation (see below).\n*''Do you use bones or morph targets?'' We use bones. At the highest level of detail there are approximately 55 bones. Obviously, at lower resolutions a smaller subset of bones can be used that is more appropriate. Morph targets are easily derived from bone poses (sample code is available).\n[img[microposes|poses.png]]\n*''What expressions can the head make?'' ~DMKs contain pose information to make animation more flexible. Bone based 'micro-poses' (eye blinks, winks, smiles, speech visemes, pupil changes, silly expressions, tongue movement, etc) are easy to combine with fractional weights to produce vast numbers of expressions. Example code to do this is included with the DMSDK. Developers can either use the ready made poses, make their own, or just use their own animation system.\n*''Can I modify the head?'' Yes, once the head is extracted with the DMSDK and in your engine, you can do what you like to it. We've added a few extra features to the head to make modification easy to do in code (see landmark points and reference map below).\n*''Can I add my own 3D models to the head (hats, hair, etc)?'' The DMSDK provides "landmark" points. Each head mesh is a different shape (no two people are the same). To enable neck-to-body fitting, landmark points enable you to get a handle on the head. Landmark points are also useful for fitting accessories like spectacles, head-gear, piercings, etc.\n*''I don't like / don't want the default hair. What can I do about it?'' If the hair that the user chooses in the 'Launch Pad' isn't appropriate for your game, then just ignore it. The hair geometry is just another branch in the head's scene-graph. You can add your own design (see landmark points above). The same applies to the default specs and hat.\n*''What about painting onto the skin texture?'' A standard head reference map is included to allow the developer to exactly identify points / areas on the head texture and geometry. With this feature it is possible for artists to give the head a "black eye", knock out a tooth, add a tattoo, piercings, or even apply make-up. The modification happening in 'reference-mapspace' can be transformed in code to the player's head at runtime.\n*''How much CPU time does the DMSDK take away from my game engine?'' None at all. Well, OK, it takes some CPU time while the data is being extracted from the head. But once it's in your scene-graph the DMSDK is shut down, and out of the way (if you cache the result – see below – this could be a one off operation).\n*''How do I access the Digimask head data?'' The DCP saves the head data in a “.DMK” file. This data is referred to as the DMK. You load the DMK into a buffer and pass it to the DMSDK library. The DMK data is approximately 300KB. The size varies between heads.\n*''I'm writing an online game, how do I transfer the player's head across the net?'' You could send the whole of the DMK data, but we don't recommend that. When you import the head you'll probably be doing it at a less than the maximum resolution. The texture size is also likely to be lower than the maximum available. As a result, the head's data storage size in your engine will be smaller than the DMK. So, why not just transfer the data from within your engine.\n*''We want to display a thumbnail of the player.'' The Digimask head has a thumbnail image embedded in it. It's a cropped version of the original front photo used to make the head. As such it will have the original background in it. If you want a 'clean' image, why not just render out a single frame, front-on, of the head.\n*''Can I cache the player's head in my own format?'' Yes. When a player imports their Digimask head into your game, why not cache their head in your engine's own format with their save-game data. This could make the importing of the Digimask head (and the firing up of the DMSDK) a once only operation.\n*''This sounds like hard work''. The DMSDK is distributed with sample code to get you started and to help you evaluate the DMSDK. We're never more than an email away if you need help. We develop our own custom tools in-house to help shortcut the integration process. If these might be of some use to you, let us know. All you have to do is ask. We keep all information we receive from you in strictest confidence.
